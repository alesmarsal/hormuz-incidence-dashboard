Importers paid USD 533 bn more for oil at pre-war volumes; the Gulf eight gained 28 bn, other exporters 327 bn, and 225 bn went with the barrels not shipped

Change in each country's net oil (petroleum liquids) and gas trade receipts or payments against the pre-war path (baseline prewar_eia_steo_202602+gas_prewar_202602), cumulated from March 2026. Combined: realised prices and volumes for the 13 exporters measured exactly; for every other country, pre-war net trade (EIA, 2025) at the change in price (first order). This is the first-round income transfer through trade, not welfare and not GDP: importers' quantity responses, freight, refined-product margins and financial channels are left out. Exporters and importers are classified by pre-war net liquids trade; the Gulf eight are Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, Iraq and Iran. Barrels not shipped: the 13 exporters' realised receipts less their first-order receipts (the value of the barrels the closure kept off the market, mostly Gulf). Volumes not adding up: EIA's world production exceeds consumption by 1.5 mb/d in 2025, and the 13's pre-war paths sit above their 2025 levels. Grade differentials: the 13 sell at their grade prices, everyone else is priced at Brent. Gas imbalance: importers are charged at the regional benchmark, but exporters outside the benchmark regions (Algeria, Turkmenistan, Azerbaijan, Nigeria, Trinidad and Tobago, Mozambique) and the US liquefaction margin are not credited. Gas runs to Sep 2026.

How to read it. First order = pre-war net trade at war prices: for a country free to choose its quantities it bounds an exporter's gain from below and an importer's loss from above; it is wrong where the closure rationed barrels, which is why the 13 exporters are measured exactly in the combined layer. Gas: Europe (excluding Russia, Belarus, the Caucasus and Central Asia) at the European benchmark, East Asia, the Pacific and South Asia at LNG Japan, the United States, Canada and Mexico at Henry Hub; countries where no benchmark plausibly applies (pipeline contracts, regulated prices) are left out (grey). Hubs (Singapore, the Netherlands, Belgium, Malta, Panama, Gibraltar) are treated as consumers although part of their imports are re-exported or sold as bunkers. 186 countries with 2025 GDP and EIA trade data are on the map; territories without IMF GDP are off the map but in the sums. Small states may not be drawn at world scale; they are in the table. Basemap: Plotly's world map, derived from Natural Earth (public domain); boundaries are not an endorsement.
