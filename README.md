# Who gained and who lost from the 2026 oil shock?

A work-in-progress data dashboard: **https://alesmarsal.github.io/hormuz-incidence-dashboard/**

The 2026 war closed the Strait of Hormuz for months. Oil prices doubled, but Gulf exporters lost much of their export
volume. The dashboard tracks two questions with public data:

- **A. Saudi Arabia.** How large was the oil windfall once lost export volume is netted out, and how was it used?
- **B. 13 oil exporters** (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman, Iraq, Iran; United States, Norway, Canada,
  Brazil, Russia). How exposed are they to oil prices, and where did the 2026 shock leave each of them?

**Status.** Preliminary results, revised as new data are released. Oil to October 2026; natural gas and LNG to
September 2026 (monthly for Qatar, the United States, Norway and Canada; Russia and Oman at 2024 volumes; not yet for
the UAE and Iran). The "Sensitivity" page shows how receipts and budgets moved with the oil price in 2000–2025 (export
grade prices and OPEC members' export values from OPEC's Annual Statistical Bulletin 2026). The "Arithmetic" page
shows the cumulative paths since March 2026, the break-even rule (share of barrels lost against the price rise) and the
price rule against the outcome.

**Data.** Public statistical releases only: OPEC Monthly Oil Market Report, U.S. EIA (Short-Term Energy Outlook,
International Energy Statistics, spot prices), JODI (oil and gas), UN Comtrade, World Bank commodity prices, IMF World Revenue
Longitudinal Database, OPEC Annual Statistical Bulletin, Statistics Norway, Russia's Ministry of Finance, IMF World
Economic Outlook, Saudi Central Bank (SAMA), Saudi
Ministry of Finance, GASTAT, and national sources for Oman. Each page lists its sources down to the table.
