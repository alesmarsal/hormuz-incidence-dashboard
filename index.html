Who gained and who lost from the 2026 oil shock?

The 2026 war closed the Strait of Hormuz for months. Oil prices doubled, but Gulf exporters lost much of their export volume. This dashboard tracks two questions with public data, updated as new releases come out.
Question A · Saudi Arabia

How large was Saudi Arabia's oil windfall once lost export volume is netted out, and how was it used?

So far: crude export receipts were USD +24.3 bn above the pre-war path in March–Oct 2026 (+1.91% of 2025 GDP), but only +6.5 bn above the same months of 2025. Official reserves were SAR 108.4 bn lower than in February.

Question B · 13 oil exporters

How exposed are oil exporters to oil prices, and where did the 2026 shock leave each of them?

So far: Comparators gained USD 216 bn, the eight Gulf exporters together 28 bn (March–Oct 2026); Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain and Iraq lost under every baseline. The two most exposed, Kuwait (-2.8% of GDP) and Iraq (-1.1%), lost; correlation of exposure with outcome -0.34. With gas, Qatar's loss reaches 19% of GDP (oil alone 6%); its LNG exports fell 80% from March; Norway gains 7% (to Sep).
